# Future Case Study Charter — Foromni and Internet Embassy (IE)

**Status: QUEUED FUTURE RESEARCH, NOT AUTHORIZED FOR CROSS-PROJECT ACCESS OR PUBLICATION.**  
**Founder direction, 2026-11-21:** When evidence warrants it, investigate Foromni/Internet Embassy as an illustrative case study and publish the finished report **privately inside IE as a research post**, with appropriate backlinks and attribution. This document is a Foromni-side reminder, **not** consent to access or mutate IE now.

## Purpose

Compare two separately governed projects' approaches to roadmap/relationship management, agent registry, software development convergence, GitHub lifecycle, evidence and organizational learning. Explain what the projects can teach each other and what should *remain separate*. Unlike external OSS case studies, cross-project sources may be confidential; privacy and consent take priority over backlinks, publicity or claims of collaboration.

## Trigger to prepare an actual study (future hinge, not calendar schedule)

- Foromni has a clearly approved reference architecture and at least one reproducible, independently reviewed E0 implementation or explicitly labels untested architecture as proposal;
- IE has relevant actual integration evidence and permission to access precisely named IE assets, without guessing from previous conversations;
- founder explicitly authorizes a **new read-only cross-project research scope** and specifies what may be quoted;
- editor/owner confirms a **private IE post destination**, visibility controls and who may read it;
- citation and claim review checks actual source/version, avoids private keys, customer/user details, personal information and unapproved code disclosures;
- founder gives separate final approval before any IE write or publication.

If these conditions are not met, keep the study queued. A private post can still leak through wrong audience, embeds, notifications, search indexing or backlinks; the actual visibility must be verified rather than assumed.

## Proposed case-study questions

1. How do roadmap and relationship management assign work, dependencies and ownership?
2. How does each project identify persistent agents across sessions and shared GitHub/provider credentials?
3. Which evidence distinguishes proposals, commits, tested features, integrated mainline, releases and actual outcomes?
4. What separation-of-duty mechanisms exist in each project, not merely diagrams?
5. How does convergence-first work affect rework, lead time, accepted functionality per full cost and architectural drift?
6. Where does one project have a reusable lesson for the other, and where would integration create unnecessary coupling?
7. What can be shared with a wider open-source learning community without exposing private architecture or third-party rights?

- Foromni research manuscript with precise source-linked evidence and limitations.

- Balanced lessons for both projects; no assertion of single-organization governance or shared production infrastructure.
- Corrections and review record for any claims that change over time.
