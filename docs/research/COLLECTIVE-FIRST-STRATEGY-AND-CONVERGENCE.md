# Collective-First Foromni: From Code Convergence to Shared Human Outcomes

**Status:** Research and strategic recommendation, not an approved architecture or development authorization  
**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Scope:** Foromni research only. No IE task, repo, data, code, integration or content publication changes.  
**Companion research:** GLOBAL-AGENT-ROADMAP-EXECUTION.md, CONVERGENCE-CONTRACT-V0.1.md, HYBRID-STACK-ADOPT-EXTEND-REJECT.md, AINO-EDUTAINMENT-LEARNING-BRIEF.md.

## Executive conclusion

**Recommend a collective-first framing, not an Internet-Embassy-first framing.** Foromni's North Star is a global network where individuals, professionals, communities, businesses, nonprofits, project teams and independently operated AI agents discover one another, coordinate meaningful work, contribute, learn and create reusable value. IE may be an independent early adopter or illustrative case *after separate authorization*; it should not define the protocol, permissions, governance, benchmark corpus, incentives or success measures.

The emerging core utility is better called **verifiable collective coordination and convergence**: convert many independent intentions and contributions into a consent-based, traceable, independently accepted outcome. The code-mainline use case is one demanding reference domain, not the full scope of Foromni.

This direction already fits the Founding Charter and the proposed PRODUCT-BOUNDARIES-AND-SHARED-CAPABILITIES document: independent participation, human agency, openness to projects beyond any one business, portable identity/provenance, least privilege, and no assumption of shared ownership. Keep those boundaries; do not silently turn a strategic proposal into binding governance.

## 1. Reframe the core problem

**Narrow framing:** How can 10,000 agents ship verified code into one protected main branch?

**Collective framing:** How can diverse people and autonomous tools, across time zones and independent organizations, discover common goals, agree on bounded commitments, divide work, share evidence, resolve conflicts, and reliably produce accepted outcomes **without surrendering their own identity, rights, data, economic interests or infrastructure**?

Most collective work has no Git branch, build test, SHA or deployable artifact. It may produce a verified research synthesis, practical educational resource, local volunteer event, design proposal, professional referral, accepted open-source contribution, mutually approved community decision, or an operated service. **Never generalize a software CI pass into proof of non-code outcomes.**

One project can have a protected canonical result while contributors retain their own independently governed branches, repositories, resources and relationships.

## 2. Five overlapping modes of value

| Mode | Participant goal | Example work | What credible acceptance means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal discovery and learning | Explore interests, develop skills, find relevant people | Learning group, mentorship, practical project | Participant confirms goals, appropriate assessment of stated competencies; consent for any shared record |
| Professional collaboration | Find collaborators, demonstrate work, build reputation | Portfolio, knowledge article, joint project, reusable template | Deliverable accepted by explicit owner/stakeholders with artifact and scope provenance |
| Community and civic cooperation | Solve shared problems and steward commons | Local initiative, translation, accessibility, policy proposal | Authorized participants agree decision criteria; evidence of execution; safeguard minority rights and privacy |
| Research and knowledge commons | Produce accurate, reusable knowledge | Source review, replicated finding, curated dataset | Traceable sources, conflicts recorded, subject-matter review; revisions and retractions supported |
| Technical/AINO coordination | Reliably coordinate software and automation | Verified code fix, service integration, structured workflow | Reproducible tests, exact artifact revisions, independent QA, protected integration and release separation |

These are **candidate domains**, not confirmed demand or built features. Participants can move between modes without needing separate identities or being forcibly assigned a commercial persona.

## 3. One portable convergence core; several proof-of-outcome adapters

Stable core contract concepts:
- **Collective / project:** independently owned governance charter, eligible participants, purpose, scope and rights.
- **Goal / proposal:** why an outcome matters, affected stakeholders, success criteria and dissent/alternatives.
- **Task / commitment:** stable ID, scoped work, custodian, due/review time, dependencies and authorization.
- **Attempt / participant:** human or agent contribution with separate execution/claim state, consent and trust level.
- **Artifact / evidence:** versioned result and source/provenance; private/public classification; no forced public upload.
- **Independent verification / decision:** who may accept which claim under which policy and evidence.
- **Convergence event:** the accepted outcome or canonical decision, with supersession, appeal/reopen and rollback/repair paths.
- **Learning record:** opt-in documentation of what worked, failed, who can reuse it and under what permissions.

Different **outcome adapters** implement domain-specific standards:
1. **Software:** Git commits, exact SHA, CI, merge-group checks, integration.
2. **Research:** sources, methods, replication, expert review, version/retraction.
3. **Learning:** goals, learner assessment, instructor/peer feedback, voluntary evidence sharing.
4. **Services/professional work:** agreed scope, client acceptance, milestones, rights, dispute processes.
5. **Collective decisions:** proposal history, authorized electorate/participation, quorum or deliberative process where applicable, transparent dissent and effective date.
6. **Community project:** consented commitments, event outcomes, receipts/observations as appropriate, impact limitations.

**Do not impose one global reputation score or generic consensus vote across all projects.** Every project must define who can decide, on what grounds, for which resources, subject to baseline safety and consent.

## 4. Participation design: people are not just agent task workers

Offer opt-in modes:
- Observer/learner: explore public projects and useful knowledge without forced registration where feasible.
- Member/contributor: publish preferences and capabilities voluntarily; propose and submit work within clear permissions.
- Project owner/maintainer: define goals, allocate tasks, admit contributions, govern owned resources.
- Independent human/AI operator: expose capability contracts; use scoped grants; keep source systems and private data under its control.
- Steward/reviewer: perform bounded independent review; disclose conflicts of interest.
- Organization/community: steward its own projects, membership policies and data-sharing rules.

Personal and professional interests may overlap, but should not be merged by default. A person can have private interests, public work samples, a professional profile, and an organization role with distinct visibility and authorization.

**Identity portability** is not compulsory real-name verification. Support pseudonymous public participation for suitable activities; require appropriate verification only for sensitive actions, payment, regulated activities, or community-specific rules. Preserve provenance without leaking private identity.

## 5. Collective discovery and matching (research hypothesis)

The network's leverage may come from matching **goals ↔ interests ↔ demonstrated skills ↔ available capabilities ↔ unmet needs**, with user-controlled discovery.

Research mechanisms:
- Interest/capability declarations with granular visibility and expiry.
- Public and opt-in discovery of projects, tasks, learning circles and resource needs.
- Participant-selected goals and availability—not automated imposition of tasks.
- Verified claims grounded in permitted public artifacts, not AI-generated reputational speculation.
- Recommendation explanations, opt-out, no forced engagement, protection against bias and spam.
- Human-to-human, human-to-agent and agent-to-agent handoffs under the same authorization and evidence vocabulary.

Risk: turning people into a supply pool for other actors' goals. Counterweight: contributor-controlled permissions, transparent expectations, meaningful decline/revoke, benefit-sharing agreements when needed, and governance by the affected collective.

## 6. Collective governance is a distinct first-class problem

Code merge queues decide *technical admissibility*. They do not decide whose collective goals are legitimate or who has a right to speak for others.

Foromni must separate:
- **Proposal and deliberation** (any authorized interested party may contribute ideas);
- **Decision legitimacy** (project-specific mandate, consent, quorum/vote or maintainership, plus appeals as appropriate);
- **Execution delegation** (bounded authority and revocable grants);
- **Independent review** (outcome evidence and conflict handling);
- **Resource ownership** (money, code, IP, datasets, user records);
- **Release/publication** (the correct owner approves consequences).

Source candidates to study:
- **Decidim:** open-source participatory decision-making, proposals, results and transparent collective processes — https://docs.decidim.org/en/develop/features/general-description.html
- **Loomio:** structured group discussion and documented decisions — https://www.loomio.com/docs/en/
- **Open Collective:** fiscal hosting/transparent project expense infrastructure (not a universal revenue-split or worker-rights system) — https://oscollective.org/projects/
- **Open source participation guidance:** maintainer permission, community practices and accepted contributions — https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides/participating-in-open-source-communities
- **OECD platform cooperatives review:** coordination benefits and scale/participation limits, not proof that a cooperative is the right legal form for Foromni — https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/09/empowering-communities-with-platform-cooperatives_63d716b6/c2ddfc9f-en.pdf

These are research inputs, not vendor selections or adopted governance rules.

## 7. Value and incentives without capture

Competing motivations coexist: curiosity, learning, community service, professional visibility, compensation, ownership, belonging and access to useful infrastructure. No single incentive (especially a universal token, score, or payment scheme) should dominate.

Potential consent-based forms:
- Attribution and verifiable portfolios with correction rights.
- Mentorship, skill demonstrations and learning progression.
- Contribution acknowledgements, maintainership invitations, reusable assets.
- Paid professional tasks only with clearly agreed budgets, rights, acceptance, disputes and payments.
- Voluntary sponsorship/fiscal hosting where appropriate.
- Non-monetary shared public benefit.

**Deferred decisions:** financial incentives, platform fees, tokens, pooled revenue, reputation markets, intellectual-property assignment, ownership/equity, payout programs and legal structure require independent research and explicit authorization. Never promise returns or infer that a contribution transfers copyright.

Use outcome quality, participant retention, agency/consent, and fair distribution of benefits as indicators, not mere engagement.

## 8. Four collective use cases to test on paper before software

### Case A: Independent open-source accessibility initiative
A contributor proposes a fix, an established maintainer owns the target repo, agents can draft/test isolated patches, an independent reviewer checks accessibly and security, upstream owner decides acceptance. Foromni may record the outcome but cannot force upstream merge.

### Case B: Personal skill-building group
People opt into a study circle, local older-model tutors assist, a mentor checks learning outputs, participants choose whether to publish portfolios. A tutor's self-report never counts as qualification evidence.

### Case C: Global research collaboration
Three independent groups review sources in different languages, maintain contested findings, document uncertainty, invite replication, publish a consented synthesis and preserve minority/contrary evidence. No Git-mainline assumption.

### Case D: Professional/community service initiative
A neighborhood association or small business defines a bounded problem; volunteers, specialists and agents coordinate tasks; stakeholders accept deliverables; privacy and payment obligations follow their own policies. Foromni does not assume a contract or partnership exists.

## 9. Comparative strategic tests: collective-first vs IE-centric vs purely technical

| Criterion | Collective-first | IE-centric | Code-convergence-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fits stated founding mission | Strong | Incomplete | Incomplete |
| Independence from one business | Strong by design | Weak | Moderate |
| Personal and professional relevance | Strong potential, needs validation | Customer/member shaped | Mainly developer shaped |
| Ease of measurable early prototype | Harder; diverse acceptance | Easier inside one organization | Strong for CI/merge checks |
| Governance/privacy complexity | High | Lower apparent, but business-specific | Technical/supply-chain risks |
| Interoperability potential | Broad, if contract remains modular | Risks IE-specific assumptions | Excellent technical base but narrow |
| Risk of scope sprawl | High; demands discipline | Lower | Lower |
| Recommended role | **North Star and research frame** | Optional independent case, no current integration | **First demanding technical proof domain** |

**Important nuance:** A collective-first mission should not mean building all personal/community features immediately. Keep initial technical research centered on a smallest useful, falsifiable convergence test, while defining portable abstractions so the protocol can extend to other domains later.

## 10. Research priorities and evaluation plan

**C0 — Mission/contract reconciliation (now, documents only):** map current charter, roadmap, domain model and convergence research into a stable cross-domain vocabulary; record contradictions and open decisions.

**C1 — Participant research:** learn from at least 3 types of independently governed collectives (a project maintainer, professional collaboration and interest/community group). Validate needs, consent expectations and acceptance criteria; do not fabricate interviews or endorsements.

**C2 — Five-domain case mapping:** map exact stage/authority/evidence decisions for software, knowledge/research, learning, community governance and professional work; identify which core fields survive unchanged and which require domain-specific plugins.

**C3 — Governance and privacy tabletop:** impersonation, disputes, minority dissent, involuntary publication, unauthorized agent actions, conflict of interest, contributor withdrawal, confidential source re-use, and contaminated outcomes.

**C4 — Small synthetic proof (requires separate build authorization):** two collectives, different decision rules, an older self-hosted model, a cloud agent and a human contributor; one software task, one non-code task; both need independently authorized completion and revision-safe handoff.

**C5 — Platform selection (later):** test combinations of Decidim/Loomio-style governance, roadmap planning, agent transport, open Git/CI integration and provenance ledger. Adopt/extend/reject rather than replicate mature platforms.

## 11. Success measures

- **Meaningful completed outcomes**, verified against the right domain-specific agreement.
- Contributor-reported autonomy, fair treatment and ability to withdraw.
- Time from proposal to accepted deliverable, broken down by human and automated steps.
- Independent reviewer confidence and documented disagreements/appeals.
- Cross-organization reuse without transfer of ownership.
- Reproducible contribution provenance and permissions; no unsupported green status.
- Data protection, revocation and visibility errors (target zero in controlled tests).
- Sustainable cost per accepted outcome, not per generated task.
- Community health: repeat collaborations, high-quality mentorship, accepted external work—not follower count alone.

Metrics are **proposed**, not current product measurements.

## 12. Decisions and guardrails

- Use **Foromni collective potential** as the public research frame and long-term design beneficiary.
- Treat IE as one *possible* independent member organization, not a test dependency or privileged source of truth.
- Include personal and professional interests equally; neither should require participation in a commercial network.
- Preserve autonomy and explicit ownership for every collective and person.
- Keep human/legal decision rights distinct from model capability.
- Accept the Convergence Contract as a research hypothesis pending formal review, not as deployed infrastructure.
- Maintain research-only scope; no new platform build, IE work, outreach, content publication, product merge, or permission changes.

**Central question:** Can independent participants create more verified shared value through Foromni while retaining meaningful control over their own work, identity and relationships?

Foromni's founding motto remains apt: **Build today, so others can enjoy tomorrow.**
