# Foromni Ecosystem Participation Model

**Status:** Foundational proposal for discussion and refinement.

## Purpose

Foromni should grow through reciprocal participation, not by attempting to own or rebuild everything. Its strength should come from connecting independent people, projects, businesses, communities, open-source maintainers, and intelligent systems in ways that create durable value for all participants.

The network should both contribute outward and welcome contributions inward.

## The hybrid strategy: build, reuse, contribute, connect

Foromni follows four complementary paths.

### 1. Build shared capabilities

Develop open, reusable capabilities where a genuine ecosystem gap exists: interoperability patterns, contribution records, discovery, coordination, agent authorization, portable workflows, documentation, and reference implementations.

Build only where there is a clear user need or a strategic gap. Prefer small, composable components over a monolithic platform.

### 2. Reuse and integrate existing work

Before creating a new tool or standard:

- Discover existing projects and their communities.
- Read their documentation, license, governance, roadmap, and contribution guidance.
- Evaluate maturity, security posture, maintenance activity, interoperability, and fit.
- Prefer adoption, configuration, extension, or integration when that serves participants better than rebuilding.
- Preserve upstream attribution and comply with licenses and trademarks.

Foromni should be a connector and multiplier for the wider ecosystem, not a competing copy of every useful project.

### 3. Contribute upstream

Find specific, useful ways to help existing projects:

- Fix reproducible bugs.
- Improve documentation and onboarding.
- Add tests or examples when authorized by the upstream project's norms.
- Improve accessibility, security, interoperability, and developer experience.
- Share integrations and lessons learned.
- Sponsor or otherwise support maintainers only through explicitly approved budgets.

Start with the project's contribution process. Open respectful issues or discussions before proposing substantial changes. Do not spam maintainers, presume endorsement, or present a contribution as a partnership before the other party agrees.

### 4. Invite reciprocal participation

Make it easy for independent projects, businesses, developers, and communities to contribute capabilities, integrations, knowledge, and proposals to Foromni.

Offer clear value in return:

- Visibility and attribution.
- Better interoperability and distribution.
- Reusable tools and reference implementations.
- Access to collaborators and complementary capabilities.
- Shared learning and documented opportunities.
- A voice in relevant open standards and roadmap discussions.

Participation should not require exclusivity, transferring intellectual property, joining Internet Embassy, or surrendering control of an independent project.

## The relationship lifecycle

1. **Discover:** Identify a relevant project, maintainer, business, or community.
2. **Understand:** Study its mission, needs, governance, license, technical contracts, and existing work before reaching out.
3. **Map mutual value:** Identify a concrete contribution or integration that benefits both sides.
4. **Connect respectfully:** Contact the appropriate maintainers through their preferred public channels. Explain the specific value and ask before assuming collaboration.
5. **Contribute:** Deliver a bounded, reviewable contribution under the recipient project's rules.
6. **Document:** Record the public contribution, outcome, attribution, and lessons learned.
7. **Invite:** Where there is genuine mutual interest, invite the participant to publish an integration, capability, proposal, or contribution to Foromni.
8. **Maintain:** Keep the relationship useful through follow-through, transparent communication, and ongoing reciprocal value.

A relationship is not a metric to collect. The goal is trust and useful collaboration over time.

## Shared development and testing model

Foromni may develop experimental capabilities in its own isolated repositories, branches, or sandbox environments. These environments allow rapid iteration without imposing instability on the public network or on Internet Embassy.

Use this progression:

**Explore → Prototype → Isolate → Validate → Document → Offer upstream or publish → Integrate deliberately**

- Experiments remain clearly labeled as experimental.
- Testing and hosted compute follow the applicable project authorization and cost controls.
- Do not connect experimental systems to production data or credentials.
- Record dependencies, licenses, provenance, known limitations, and security considerations.
- Promote only reusable, appropriately licensed, reviewed capabilities.
- Preserve rollback paths and version compatibility.

### Relationship with Internet Embassy

Internet Embassy can be an early adopter, implementation partner, and proving ground for selected Foromni capabilities.

The direction is reciprocal:

- Foromni can develop open patterns, standards, reference components, and shared infrastructure.
- Internet Embassy can implement, operate, and provide feedback on selected capabilities in its own products.
- Improvements that are suitable for the common ecosystem can be contributed back under clear ownership and licensing terms.
- Other independent organizations should be able to adopt the same capabilities without becoming IE customers or affiliates.

Foromni must not become a back door for importing private IE code, customer data, credentials, or proprietary business logic into public repositories. Each contribution must have clear provenance, permission, and license compatibility.

## Intellectual property, licensing, and attribution

Before copying, adapting, or accepting code, data, documentation, models, or other assets:

- Identify the source and applicable license or permission.
- Confirm compatibility with the destination project's license.
- Preserve required notices and attribution.
- Do not assume public visibility means permission to relicense or republish.
- Keep proprietary, customer-owned, and confidential material separate.
- Document ownership and contribution terms for substantial contributions.

Foromni should select explicit repository and asset licenses through a separate maintainer decision before soliciting broad external code contributions. Until then, do not imply that all repository contents are open for unrestricted reuse.

## Governance and trust

- Independent projects retain their own governance and roadmaps.
- No partnership, endorsement, or affiliation is implied without mutual confirmation.
- Do not make commitments on behalf of another project.
- Record material collaboration decisions and conflicts of interest transparently.
- Use least-privilege access and human approval for consequential cross-project actions.
- Respect maintainer capacity, community norms, privacy, and security boundaries.

## Early success measures

Prefer evidence of useful outcomes over vanity metrics:

- Upstream contributions accepted and maintained.
- Useful integrations adopted by independent projects.
- Repeat collaborations and reciprocal contributions.
- Time saved or capabilities gained by participants.
- Reusable components with clear documentation and provenance.
- External contributors who can onboard and deliver without founder intervention.
- Trust, responsiveness, and long-term maintenance health.

Do not optimize for raw repository count, unsolicited outreach volume, stars, or superficial partnership announcements.

## Initial execution priorities

1. Create a discovery inventory of aligned open-source projects and communities.
2. Record each project's purpose, license, governance, activity, contribution process, and potential points of interoperability.
3. Select a small number of high-fit projects for careful study.
4. Identify one genuinely useful, bounded upstream contribution per selected project.
5. Establish a reusable project relationship brief and contribution record.
6. Publish a clear path for external projects to propose integrations and contributions to Foromni.
7. Pilot the model with Internet Embassy while preserving organizational and intellectual-property boundaries.

---

**Build together. Contribute upstream. Keep ownership clear. Let value compound.**
